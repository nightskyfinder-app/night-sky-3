    Guide

    How to use Night Sky Finder

    Full guide ↗
    Looking around

    Drag the sky to turn and tilt. Pinch to zoom. Tap anything to learn what it is; a name always opens its own card.

    The clock at the top right is a time machine: tap it to see the sky hours or days from now, or press Play to watch it turn.

    Pointing your phone at the sky

    Tap the phone button (bottom right) and hold your phone up. The map follows wherever you aim. The camera button lays the map over your camera.

    If things look shifted left or right, wave the phone in a figure-8 to calibrate the compass. Still off? Line it up with something real:

    Finding things

    Search (magnifier) finds planets, stars, constellations, galaxies and satellites like the ISS. Pick one and a gold arrow at the edge of the screen leads you to it.

    Tonight (star) lists what's worth seeing: the Moon, planets, space station passes, meteor showers and the best deep-sky sights.

    Weather and stargazing rating

    The cloud button shows current weather and an hour-by-hour stargazing rating for tonight. Its dot is the rating: green is great, yellow is fair, red is poor.

    Daytime: see what's behind the daylight

    In daytime the sky turns blue, just like outside. Open Layers and slide Daylight to the left to fade it away and see the stars and planets above you. Slide it back to the right for the real sky.

    Never point binoculars or a telescope at the Sun.

    Satellites and the space station

    Satellites appear as white dots with a short line showing their path. A bright dot is sunlit and can be seen with your eyes; a faint ring is in Earth's shadow and is invisible right now.

    Satellites are easiest to see in the first two hours after sunset and before sunrise. They move steadily and never blink.

    Planes

    Turn on Planes in Layers to see live aircraft as small blue plane shapes, with their flight number and height. Planes blink with red, green and white lights; satellites don't.

    Planes show live only, so they hide while you're time traveling.

    Planes need a one-time setup: a small relay address goes in Layers. The full guide explains how.

    "What's that moving light?"

    Put the moving light in the center ring and tap the moving-light button (bottom right). The app tells you which plane or satellite it is. If the light isn't moving, tap it on the map instead: it's a star or planet.

    Layers, red mode and offline

    Layers chooses what's drawn: constellation lines, names, galaxies, the Milky Way, satellites, planes and weather.

    Red mode (crescent) tints the screen red so your eyes stay used to the dark.

    Add the app to your home screen and it works with no signal. Satellites, planes and weather need a connection now and then.

    Privacy

    Your exact location stays on your phone. Weather and planes send only a rough position, rounded to a few miles. Satellite data doesn't send any location. Nothing is tied to your name, and the app has no account.
